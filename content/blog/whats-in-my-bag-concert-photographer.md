Gear lists are usually aspirational — what someone *would* bring if the bag had no weight limit. This is the opposite: the actual kit I carry for North Sea Jazz, MOJO, Radio 538 and club shows across the Netherlands. Everything in here has earned its place across a lot of festival days.

## Two bodies: Canon EOS R5 Mark II × 2

I shoot a pair of [Canon EOS R5 Mark II](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=canon+eos+r5+mark+ii) bodies. Two identical bodies, for two reasons:

- **No lens swaps in the pit.** Three songs is not the time to be changing glass in the dark while beer rains down. Wide zoom on one body, tele on the other.
- **Redundancy.** When a label or festival hires you, "my camera broke" is not a sentence you're allowed to say. The second body is the insurance policy that pays for itself the first time you need it.

Why this body: the stacked 45MP sensor crops far enough for editors, silent shooting without LED banding, and AF that holds a singer through haze and backlight. Real-world reference: my shots of Kane at Ahoy were ISO 2500, f/2.8, 1/800s — clean, straight from the pit. Full camera recommendations for every budget are in [my concert camera guide](/blog/best-cameras-for-concert-photography).

## The glass

### Canon RF 15-35mm f/2.8L IS USM

The [RF 15-35mm f/2.8](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=canon+rf+15-35mm+f2.8) is my pit workhorse. At the barrier you're two meters from the artist — 15mm turns that proximity into drama: full stage, light rig and crowd energy in one frame. Most of my portfolio comes off this lens.

### Canon RF 70-200mm f/2.8L IS USM Z

The other body carries the [RF 70-200mm f/2.8 Z](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=canon+rf+70-200mm+f2.8+z) — the newer black, internal-zoom version. Faces, drummers, and festival stages where the pit is ten meters out. The internal zoom matters more than it sounds: nothing extends, so it handles fast and stays balanced all night.

### Canon RF 24-70mm f/2.8L IS USM

The [24-70mm f/2.8](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=canon+rf+24-70mm+f2.8) completes the trinity. In the pit the 15-35/70-200 pair does most of the work, but for events, corporate work and backstage this is the lens that stays on.

### Canon RF 10-20mm f/4L IS STM

The [RF 10-20mm f/4L](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=canon+rf+10-20mm+f4) is for the shots where even 15mm isn't wide enough: barrier shots that swallow the whole stage, the light rig and the crowd in a single frame. It's f/4, so it's a lens for the big lighting moments rather than the dark verses — but at 10mm, nothing else delivers this perspective.

### Canon RF 100-500mm f/4.5-7.1L IS USM

The [RF 100-500](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=canon+rf+100-500mm) is the specialist: big festival stages, shooting from the FOH tower, and anything where the pit is a rumor. It stays in the roller until it's needed — but when it's needed, nothing else does the job.

## Cards, readers and the edit machine

- **CFexpress Type B + SD cards** — the R5 Mark II writes to both simultaneously, so every frame exists twice the moment it's taken. Client work rule: one card failure may never equal lost photos.
- **Two card readers** — because the moment your only reader dies is always the night of the deadline. Redundancy isn't just for bodies.
- **MacBook Pro, maxed out** — the mobile edit bay. Same-night delivery means culling and editing happen backstage or on the train home, not "tomorrow". A maxed-out machine earns its price back in Lightroom's AI Denoise alone.

## The small stuff that saves shows

- **Spare batteries** — more than you think you need; cold outdoor festivals eat them.
- **Earplugs** — you're next to the PA for hours, every show. Hearing damage is the one gear failure you can't replace. Non-negotiable.
- **Profoto A10** — flash is banned in virtually every pit, and stage light *is* the picture. But concert work comes with events, backstage portraits and meet & greets, and for those the [Profoto A10](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=profoto+a10) earns its (considerable) price: round-head light quality in a hot shoe flash.

## The trolley: Peak Design Roller Pro

All of it travels in the [Peak Design Roller Pro](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=peak+design+roller+pro) — the biggest quality-of-life upgrade of my recent seasons. Festival days mean trains, shuttle vans and backstage corridors; a carry-on-sized roller that swallows two bodies, five lenses and the MacBook means I arrive at the first band without my back already gone. In the pit itself it's two bodies on straps and nothing more — the roller stays in the press room.
